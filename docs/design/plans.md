# What is planned and in what order

Settled decisions, moved here from `CLAUDE.md`. Keep them current in the same PR as the change.

**Phase order (decided after 4b, revised 2026-10-29):** 5a Body data → extensibility + body sections → **Foundations (Aeronautics + Dyson Sphere counters and sky)** → 6 Asteroids → 7 Machines → 7b Aeronautics ships (Space Thruster, Helm; Aeronautics is required now) → 5b Body rendering → 8 → 9 → 10. Gameplay comes before the GeckoLib rendering phase.

**Interview decisions (2026-10-29, owner's answers; later work follows these)**
- **Order of work (revised after the brainstorm, same day):** (1) housekeeping; (2) playtest from the checklist and fix what comes back, plus the raw-item collector part and a first balance pass; (3) first person with a cockpit camera (5b-2a); (4) an **Act III vertical slice** before stations, to prove the riskiest part early: the `ruined_earth` dimension and Atmospheric Portal, the **full three-era sky**, and one Terraforming Spire pulse that rewrites biomes and resends chunks; (5) the launch complex; (6) stations stage 1, then helper drones in job order (ferry, mine, collect, carry); (7) the rest of Act III (weather, Data Monolith and villagers, restoration and the victory state); (8) the 3D assembly view (5b-2b); (9) 7b Aeronautics ships; (10) polish (goggle tooltips, Ponder, JEI freezing category, sounds) alongside whichever step has room. Every step is split into small PRs. The order may change when a step shows it needs to.

- **Helper drones** (the probe model is their blueprint) do four jobs: ferry items between stations, mine asteroids, collect from dust clouds, carry payloads to the launcher. Built unattended with defaults from 2026-10-31, one job per PR.
  - **Done, helper drones PR 1 (`helper/`, the ferry):** `HelperDrone` (a plain `Entity` + `GeoEntity` drawn with `geo/entity/mining_probe` at half size by `client/render/HelperDroneRenderer`; no gravity, moved by `setPos` at `helper.speed` blocks per tick, steered by `HelperSteering` as below, hovers one block above the inventory it works on when that cell is free), `HelperLinks` (source and destination `BlockPos`, a data component on the item and NBT on the drone), `Ferry` (pure phases IDLE, TO_SOURCE, TO_DESTINATION, WAITING with `actionOn`/`after`), `HelperDroneItem` (sneak-use on an inventory block sets the source, then the destination, a third sneak-use starts over; use releases the drone with those links; tooltip shows them). The drone loads up to `helper.carry` items of one kind from the first non-empty slots of the source's item handler, unloads into the destination's (what does not fit stays aboard), waits `helper.pause` ticks when there is nothing to take or no room, and idles without both links. Sneak-use with an empty hand takes it back as its item (links kept, cargo dropped); a hit does the same. Right-click reports its status. Carrying payloads to the launcher is this job with the Launch Controller as destination (tested). Config group `helper` (speed 0.35, carry 16, reach 1.5, pause 40). Replicator recipe (Tools, Expansion; placeholder price).
  - **Done, helper drones PR 2 (mining):** `HelperLinks` gained a `mine` point (sneak-use the item on asteroid rock; it replaces the source, a source replaces it). `Mining` (pure phases IDLE, TO_ROCK, DIGGING, TO_DESTINATION, WAITING with `actionOn`/`after`): the drone looks for the asteroid block (`#void_reclaimer:asteroid_blocks`) nearest its mine point within `helper.mineRadius` (a scan at most once per `helper.pause` ticks), hovers above it, digs for `helper.digTicks` (block particles), takes the block as its item (as the Matter Harvester lets it drop; no dust refund, no energy), carries it to the destination and comes back; with no rock in reach it waits. One kind per load, `helper.carry` at most; a different rock while carrying is dropped as an item.
  - **Done, helper drones PR 3 (collecting):** `HelperLinks` gained a `collect` point (sneak-use the item in the air: where the player stands; it replaces a source or mine point; no destination needed, the owner is the destination). `Collecting` (pure phases IDLE, TO_POINT, GATHERING, TO_OWNER, WAITING): the drone hovers a block above the point gathering the cloud's dust into its tank (`DustField.sample` density, `DustTicker.gainPerTick` as a still body, times `helper.collectRate`, capped at `helper.tank`); full, it flies to its owner (online, same level, within `helper.ownerRange`) and hands over what fits the owner's storage (`DustData.add`, `DustLedger.record`, synced); with the owner out of reach or full it waits `helper.pause` ticks and tries again. A point outside any cloud gathers nothing and keeps gathering (the status says so).
  - **Done, steering (`HelperSteering`, pure):** a drone flies straight and only looks at the cells its next step enters (a few block lookups a tick). When a block is in the way, `search` (a bounded, greedy search over free cells across their faces, at most `helper.searchNodes` cells, at most once per 10 ticks) gives straightened waypoints, kept in `Route`, not saved. A search that runs out of cells gives the way to its closest cell, always closer than where it began, so a chain of them ends; this also takes a drone round a big convex rock. With no closer cell the drone holds still for `helper.blockedRetry` ticks, says "Blocked" in its status, and looks again, with cargo and phase untouched; a target that moves more than 2 blocks ends the hold. A cell is free when its block has no collision shape, is inside the build height and its chunk is loaded (a search never loads a chunk; a Sable ship's blocks are in its plot, so drones do not see ships). The work spot is the block's first free neighbour (`workSpot`: top, north, south, west, east, under), and a drone only counts as arrived with a clear line to it, so it does not work through a wall; a block with no open face is out of reach. Miners only choose rock with an open face. A drone inside a block (one was set on it, an old save) leaves straight towards its target.

**Danger (owner, 2026-11-01; replaces "no hazards except time"):** environmental only, with still no enemies or combat. Comes after remote control. Nothing below is built yet, and each item gets its own interview and file list.
- **Done, shells:** a parked shell takes no harm; only a command that ignores invulnerability reaches one, and it leaves a wreck. While linked the shell is the player's body, and takes harm as a body does. Sneak-use scraps your own idle shell into a wreck to loot; the shell item does not come back.
- **Main body destroyed (done, `Links.leaveWreck`, `aMainBodyWreckOutlivesACrash`):** it stays as a wreck where it died and the player flies back to salvage it. Reuses the shell wreck code. Replaces today's respawn rules for what is carried. The player file and a chunk save apart, so items must reach somewhere a crash can't lose them before they leave the player: copy the order in `Links.wake`. With the keepInventory game rule nothing is left behind. (Danger page, 2026-11-01:)
  - **Where you wake:** at a Backup Core in a new starter Drone, or in a Remote Shell if you own one.
  - **What stays:** everything: inventory, sections and modules, and the items in any job it paid for (`Replicator.leftBy`: put them somewhere a crash can't lose them, then `settle`).
  - **How long:** for good, with a HUD marker. In space it drifts slowly, which suits the Wreck Finder.
- **Crashes:** a hard ship crash breaks blocks near the impact, more at higher speed. Under 5 blocks a second nothing breaks. Weak blocks go first, and at high enough speed anything can break, the Helm and Station Core included. Bodies take collision damage too. Fall damage on Earth stays.
- **Toxic air:** wears the body's parts, and a part worn to zero stops giving its stats until repaired. It never takes health.
- **Storms:** come every 2 to 3 hours by default, set in the mods menu, and begin after the Expansion milestone.
- **Landing without a module:** you may go through the portal, fall damage applies, and the portal or an AI log warns you first.
- **Hazards chosen:** debris storms (fast rock showers that chip bodies and stations), Earth's toxic air (hurts bodies until sectors heal), ion storms (scramble sensors and the HUD and weaken remote links), solar flares (a warning, then take cover behind rock or a station), fracturing asteroids (too much drilling splits a rock and sends pieces drifting), and a debris belt around Earth (a dangerous crossing that clears as the planet heals).
- **Not chosen:** radiation near stars, running out of power, dust squalls, quakes and sinkholes.

**Remote control (interview 2026-10-31, owner's answers; planned, not built; after the assembly view)**
- **Idea:** sit at a base or station with the main body and run other bodies around the galaxy. The player is an AI consciousness; a body is only somewhere it runs.
- **Take over:** built Remote Shells holding a full body (Drone, Mech or Ship), deployed in the world and inert until linked, and any Helper Drone you own. **Connect from:** an Uplink Seat block at any base or station, including a Station Core ship; the main body stays visible in it and the seat slowly charges it.
- **Range:** a relay network: the seat's base range, Relay blocks (also on stations and ships), and each Dyson tier adds range, ending near galaxy-wide. Static near the edge. A lit Atmospheric Portal joins the deep space and ruined Earth networks.
- **Link loss:** signal lost: wake at the seat, the shell goes dormant where it is, reconnectable. Shell destroyed: wake at the seat, its contents stay in a wreck. The main body is safe.
- **Mind shared, body separate:** dust, the Replicator queue, milestones and logs follow you. Each shell keeps its own items, energy, health, wear, paint and tether. While linked, dust capacity reads the home body (lore: the AI streams its dust through the network; flavour only, dust still never gives energy). The owner may later want items to move the same way. The Replicator's station bonus follows the shell. A seat's ship keeps its velocity, so range and the wake point drift with it.
- **Idle shells (owner, 2026-10-31):** a shell left in sunlight recharges slowly, the way a body does, so where you park one matters. An unloaded shell catches up from the time it was away.
- **Station seat perks:** see "Round two".
- **Done, stale shells and the outbox reader:** `link/Outboxes` gives each outbox one look per server session, holding the shell's chunk loaded with a ticket that ends by itself, and rebuilds a shell that is missing or only there as an older copy. Once a session, because only a crash loses a shell and a crash ends the session: looking again would take a shell that has moved or been destroyed for a lost one. An outbox ends after two saves of the shell's level, each of which updates where the shell is (`Outbox.saved`). A shell that is killed clears its outbox. A copy of a shell whose owner is in it, or that is older than an outbox that can be read, removes itself (`Links.stale`, checked once a second in `BodyHost.tick`) and cannot be linked to; with the owner offline nobody can tell, and nobody can use the copy either.
- **Singleplayer loads its owner from `level.dat`, not the player file.** `PlayerList.saveAll` only writes the player file, so a link in a real game was only as safe as the last autosave. `Links.saveNow` writes both. The GameTest server cannot see this: it was checked in a real client with `kill -9`, which keeps the link with the fix and loses it without.
- **Done, wrecks (`link/Wrecks`):** a shell that dies becomes a wreck in place: the same entity, with everything it carried and was built from, sections and modules included, as one list of stacks. Right-click takes out what fits; nothing goes on the floor. An emptied wreck goes. A wreck looks like the shell, cannot be hurt, and only a command that ignores invulnerability can end it.
- **Done, death in a shell:** `Links.onDeath` cancels the death at the highest priority, so nothing drops, no death marker is set and no Backup Core charge is spent, and queues a wake at the end of the tick that leaves the shell as a wreck where it was. For now the player wakes where the main body was left; the wake chain comes with the Uplink Seat.
- **Done, the shell list (`LinkState.KnownShell`):** the player file keeps one entry per shell for good: its newest link number, where it was last seen, and whether it is a wreck or gone. A shell goes on the list when it is made, and its entry is updated by every move and at every save of its level. This is what knows a shell once its outbox has ended: a crash can leave a copy from before a link in a chunk nobody visits for months, and with no outbox left, a copy older than the entry takes no link (`Links.stale`), any copy of a shell that is gone removes itself (`Links.replaced`), wrecks included, so an emptied wreck cannot come back full, and an older copy gives way when the newer one arrives in its level. While an outbox is there it decides alone, because if it cannot be read the old copy is the only body left. **Still open:** a shell destroyed with its owner offline. The list lives in the player file, so it cannot hear of that, and an outbox still running would rebuild the shell. Only a dedicated server can reach this: in singleplayer the world stops when the owner leaves. `/void_reclaimer link shells <player>` prints the list. **Two copies at once:** a level keeps one entity per id and refuses a second, and a refused copy is gone at its chunk's next save, so `Links.onEntityJoin` keeps the copy with the higher link number, whichever loaded first.
- **Done, reaching a far shell (`link/ShellReach`):** a link by id takes the shell's place from the list, loads the 3 x 3 chunks round it with a ticket that ends by itself, and moves the player when the shell turns up. Nothing changes until then, so a shell that is not there costs nothing: the list stays as it was and the link ends `NOT_FOUND`, or `TIMED_OUT` after 10 seconds. One wait per player. This is what the picker will use; `/void_reclaimer link reach <player> <id>` asks for it by hand.
- **Done, the Uplink Seat, part 1 (`link/UplinkSeatBlock`):** the block, with an id it keeps for good, and a link that remembers which seat it came from (`Link.seat`). Who sits in a seat is worked out from the players' links, not stored, so a wake by any route frees it. Right-click takes the player to the newest shell on their list that stands ready: that stands in for the picker. It has no recipe yet, so nothing in survival reaches it before the Remote Shell.
- **Done, the mirror:** while someone is linked from a seat, a `BodyHost` with the role MIRROR stands on it wearing the main body. It is built from the link's copy of the body, and only shows it: the main body is in the player file, so nothing can happen to it. It is never saved, it cannot be hurt, and the seat builds it again within a second if it goes. On a seat aboard a ship it has not been tried.
- **Done, the wake chain (`Links.wakePoint`):** a wake goes to where the main body was left. Only a dimension that is gone or locked gets past that: then a listed Backup Core, with no charge spent, because this is not a death; then the deep space spawn. A seat aboard a ship keeps the link's `from` current each tick and holds the ship loaded while it is in use (`ShipTickets`), so a wake lands where the ship has got to, and never at a position in the ship's own coordinates. The seat therefore needs no lookup by id: if its ship is unloaded or the seat is gone, `from` is its last world position.
- **Done, the seat's charge (`link/SeatCharge`):** while the mind is away the main body gains `link.seatCharge` energy a second, times `link.stationCharge` within station range of a Station Core. The main body is in the player file and not in the world, so nothing ticks it: the gain is worked out at the wake from the time the link took. A link with no seat gains nothing.
- **Done, signal range (`link/SignalMath`, `link/RelayGraph`, both pure; nothing uses them yet):** a node covers a sphere of its range. Seat 1024 and relay 2048 blocks at no Dyson tier; each tier adds its own amount to every node (2048, 16,384, 262,144, 20,000,000, adding up), so a relay chain gains from the Dyson Sphere too. A seat at a station multiplies its range by 1.5, Dyson bonus included. Static fills the outer `staticBand` of every range: strength is 1 inside it and falls in a straight line to 0 at the edge, and a seat at a station narrows the band across its whole network, because the owner's reason was to make the edge usable for longer. A chain of relays is as strong as its weakest hop, the strongest chain wins, and a lit portal pair is a bridge with no loss.
- **Done, recovering an old copy (`link/ShellRecovery`):** an old copy stands refused (`Links.stale`) and tells its owner's list where it is; the picker offers it with a second click to confirm. The newer copy's chunks load first and a shell found there is left alone. Otherwise the copy takes a number above the list's, so a lost copy that turns up later gives way to it. Reached only at a seat, by `RecoverShellPayload`.
- **Done, a lost signal counts down:** the link stands for `link.signalGrace` seconds (5) with full static and a flashing count on the HUD, and a signal back in time keeps it. Only then does the player wake.
- **Done, signal in play (`link/Signal`):** a seat refuses a shell its signal does not reach (`OUT_OF_RANGE`), before any chunk is loaded. Once a second each link from a seat is read: gone out of range, the player wakes and the shell stays where it was, ready to link again. A link with no seat has no signal rules. A seat counts as at a station by the Replicator's range. Every lit relay is a node of the graph; there are no bridges yet.
- **Done, leaving a shell by hand:** the Leave Shell key (H) asks for a wake, `client/hud/StaticLayer` draws static as the signal weakens: nothing inside the clean part of a range, and more dashes in the last share of it, through `GuiGraphics` as a GUI layer.
- **Done, the picker (`client/screen/ShellPickerScreen`):** right-clicking a seat lists every shell on the player's list that has not gone, with how far off it is and how strong the signal is there; one out of range or wrecked is shown and cannot be picked. The server builds the rows (`Signal.rows`) and checks the pick again (`PickShellPayload`: reach of the seat, then the link's own checks).
- **Done, the Relay block, part 1 (`link/RelayBlock`):** a single block that runs on Forge Energy (`link.relayFePerTick` 20, `link.relayFeBuffer` 2000, through the standard capability, nothing comes back out) and goes dark without it. A dark relay comes back with a second of energy in hand (`RelayPower`), so a short supply cycles every few seconds and does not flip the block each tick. Right-click reads it, and it has a CC peripheral. Its id lasts for good, like a seat's. **The list (`link/RelayList`, `link/Relays`):** one saved row per relay on the level: id, block, world place, and whether it was lit. A relay writes its own row once a second and at once when it changes, so a broken row mends itself. Once a second every row is held up against its block, and only a loaded chunk can drop one: a chunk that is not loaded says nothing, and its relay still counts as last seen. It uses no energy while unloaded, because nothing ticks it. A row aboard a ship names the ship, and only that ship can drop it, because another ship can take over a plot. A ship that is really removed takes its rows with it (a Sable observer, `REMOVED` and not `UNLOADED`): no block breaks to say so, and without this a removed ship's relays would count for good. **Part 2:** every lit row is a node of `RelayGraph`, in the level it is in, with the relay range and the Dyson bonus. An unloaded relay carries the signal as it was last seen. **Part 3:** a lit relay aboard a ship holds that ship loaded (`ShipTickets`, with the relay's id as the holder), so a relay ship can drift off and go on spending energy with nobody near. It lets go when it goes dark, and when it leaves the ship or is broken: Sable saves tickets, so one left behind would keep the ship loaded for good. **Part 4:** while the mind is in a shell the HUD shows four bars in the top right corner (`client/hud/SignalBarsLayer`, `SignalMath.bars`), red at the last one. The last bar goes out only when the signal is lost. **Part 5:** a Holo Projector can show the network (`setNetworkMap`, `holo <pos> network`, `display/HoloNetworkMap`, a `MAP` with the subject `network`): this dimension's relays, lit or dark, and what the lit ones reach, zoomed out to show them all, from 256 blocks up. The server draws it, so the client's dust and moving things go on top as on any map.
- **Done, the Remote Shell (`link/RemoteShellItem`):** items that each unfold into the basic body of a chassis, ahead of the player and owned by them. Which chassis is the `shell_body` component, so a datapack recipe can make a shell of any chassis. The seat and the Drone and Mech shells unlock at Expansion, the Ship shell at Starship: remote control can now be reached in survival.

- **Mechanism, reviewed against the code:** a swap, not a remote camera (`ServerPlayer.setCamera` drags the player to the camera, and flight, tools and HUD require the player to be the body). One transaction in `ServerTickEvent.Post`, teleporting before any state changes and ending in `PlayerList.saveAll()`, with a link epoch on each shell so a stale copy discards itself. **`saveAll` writes player files only:** a shell put back in the world reaches disk when its chunk is saved, so a crash in between would lose the whole body. The player file therefore keeps the shell's snapshot (an outbox with the shell's id and epoch) until the shell is known to be on disk, and rebuilds it if it is missing (found in review, PR 8). `BODY`, `ENERGY`, `TETHER`, inventory (as `Inventory.save`'s per-slot `ListTag`), health, effects, pose and velocity swap; `DUST`, `DUST_COLLECTED`, `REPLICATOR`, `BACKUP_CORES`, `DEATH_MARKER`, `FLIGHT_SETTINGS` stay. Death in a shell is cancelled at HIGHEST priority. Waking never depends on finding an entity: the seat by UUID, its last world position, a listed Backup Core with no charge spent, the deep space spawn; never a raw plot position. A seat on a ship takes a Sable force-load ticket. One entity class, `BodyHost extends LivingEntity`, with roles SHELL, WRECK and MIRROR. The PR order is in `HANDOFF.md`.

**Holo-displays (2026-10-31, the plan in `docs/HOLO_DISPLAYS_PLAN.md`)**
- **Holo Projector** (`display/HoloProjectorBlock`, `HoloProjectorBlockEntity`): shows a `display/HoloDisplay` above itself, up to 8 lines of 48 characters. The value is immutable: every change goes through `apply`, which saves and sends a packet only when something changed. A program's text switches it to text and choosing a source to a readout: the last writer wins. Sneak-use with an empty hand picks the next readout. `/void_reclaimer holo <pos> line|scale|source|clear` writes to it for checks. Replicator Blocks tab (Diversified, toggle `holoProjector`, config group `holo`, placeholder price).
- **Readouts:** `void_reclaimer:holo_readout` is a code registry of `api.holo.HoloReadout` (a sampler that reads the world into a snapshot on the server, a pure formatter). The formatters are in `display/Readouts`, the built-in kinds in `display/ReadoutKinds`. A projector refreshes every `holo.refreshTicks` (default 10). An unknown source id is kept and shows nothing.
- **Built-in readouts:** `clock`, `dyson` and `cloud` read the world; `helm`, `thruster`, `siphon`, `electrolyzer`, `spinner`, `launch_complex`, `spire` and `backup_core` read the nearest such block within `holo.sourceRange` blocks (default 8; on a ship both stand in its plot, so a projector finds what is aboard); `player` reads the nearest body within 16 blocks; `device` reads the first block beside the projector, the one below it first, that stores energy or fluid, any mod's. With nothing to read a kind shows nothing, so a projector goes blank when its machine is removed. The lines are English text composed on the server.
- **Create Display Link:** `compat/create/HoloDisplayTarget` (`create:display_target` entry `void_reclaimer:holo_projector`, attached by block entity type in `HoloDisplaySetup`). 8 rows of 48 columns. A link writes only the lines it sends, so several links can share a projector; it counts as a program writing text, so a projector showing a readout switches to text.
- **CogScript:** `mixin/CogScriptHoloMixin` adds the `holo` module (`compat/act/HoloScript`) to every program, reaching projectors within 16 blocks of the computer by their `"x,y,z"` address. Everything about the patch is in `docs/ADVANCED_COMPUTER_TECHNIC_PATCH.md`.
- **Models:** `HoloDisplay.Mode` MODEL (a GeckoLib id in `subject`: `<ns>:entity/x` means `geo/entity/x.geo.json` and `textures/entity/x.png`, the plain glow without that texture) and BODY (each viewer's own body, `BodyRenderer.drawHologram`, upright whatever way the viewer faces). Both turn slowly at about 1.5 blocks long times the scale, drawn twice: the model's texture, then a cyan wash so dark art reads as light. The server cannot know a client's models, so an unknown id shows "NO MODEL" on the client. Another program's write replaces a model: last writer wins.
- **Globes (`display/HoloGlobes`):** mode GLOBE, subject `dyson` or `earth`, turning slowly with a caption over them. The server samples `HoloGlobes.Progress` (satellites, trusses, tier, shell, the bits of `earth/SectorMap`, the sky era) at each refresh and sends it with the block only when it changed, because a client has the Dyson counters only in deep space and the era only on Earth. Dyson: a star, one dot per satellite up to 200, the shell's panels standing in a scattered order as it fills, a faint lattice from tier 3. Earth: the 4 x 4 sector grid laid on the sphere (`earth/Sectors`: columns west to east, rows north to south), ash where ruined and green where restored, every other sector a little darker so the grid shows, in an atmosphere coloured by the era. So the Earth globe is the sector map.
- **Ship miniature:** mode SHIP shows the ship the projector stands on, or else the nearest within 64 blocks, turning slowly. All on the client (`client/render/holo/HoloShips`): the ship's blocks are copied on the render thread at most every 2 seconds (4096 blocks at most), and only a copy that differs is meshed, in the background, into a `HoloMesh` (positions and texture coordinates, 60,000 vertices at most) that each frame replays with one tint. `HoloShipRegion` is the copy as a block model reads it, so the mesher never touches the live level. Block models only: what a block entity draws, such as Create's shafts, is not in the miniature. Mesh positions start from one of the ship's blocks, because a ship's plot lies millions of blocks out, past what a float holds exactly.
- **Map:** mode MAP is a top-down picture of `holo.mapRadius` blocks (default 64) each way, north up and east right. The server fills `display/HoloMapImage`, 64 x 64 packed vanilla map colours of each column's highest block, from chunks already loaded (it never loads one, so what is not loaded is blank), every `holo.mapRefreshTicks` (default 100). The picture travels only in the block's update packet and only when it changed; it is never saved, because the blocks are there to draw it again. So after a restart a map is blank for up to one refresh. The client tints the dust cloud over it (`client/render/holo/HoloMaps`, a fixed pool of 16 textures, because `RenderType.text` keeps every id it is given for good) and adds dots each frame: white for players, cyan for other living bodies and helper drones, amber for ships. At the default radius a pixel shows every second block. A projector on a ship maps its own deck, in the coordinates of its plot, so the dust and the dots are left off.
- **Drawing:** `client/render/holo/HoloProjectorRenderer`, a plain block entity renderer into the frame's shared buffers (no `endBatch`, no `Tesselator`, nothing mixed into level rendering). `HoloTransform` undoes the pose stack's own rotation so a billboard faces the viewer on a moving ship. In NeoForge 21.1 `getRenderBoundingBox` is on the renderer, not the block entity.
